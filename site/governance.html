Governance Analysis

Regulatory and monitoring analysis — a separate, interpretive layer from the evidential reports.

This section presents analysis of monitoring design and regulatory process, drawing on the evidence documented in the Reports section. Where the reports establish what has been observed, these papers examine whether existing monitoring and governance systems are structured to detect, escalate and act on findings of this kind.

This three-part series examines the architecture of environmental monitoring and assurance within UK onshore hydrocarbon regulation. The first paper develops the monitoring framework, the second applies it to the West Newton A planning application as a structured stress-mapping exercise, and the third extends the analysis to detectability, evidentiary burden and environmental assurance.

Governance papers

Monitoring, Threshold Governance and the Architecture of Assurance in UK Onshore Hydrocarbon Regulation

Governance analysisv1.0.1, July 2026

An examination of monitoring as a designed governance system rather than a neutral collection of measurements. Using Preston New Road and Preese Hall as stress-test examples, and West Newton A as a contemporary application, the paper considers detection limits, threshold logic, model stability, cross-domain segmentation and escalation. It distinguishes a system that is administratively legible from one that is necessarily complete.

Applying Monitoring Architecture: A Failure-Mode Stress Mapping of West Newton A

Governance analysisv1.0.1, July 2026

A prospective application of the monitoring-architecture framework to the West Newton A proposals. The paper maps monitoring placement, sampling cadence, thresholds, review clauses and the routes by which information moves between operational, environmental and regulatory domains. It identifies where clear exceedances would trigger defined action and where recognition of distributed or sub-threshold signals would depend upon professional synthesis.

Detectability, Evidentiary Burden and the Architecture of Environmental Assurance

Governance analysisv1.0.1, July 2026

A general analysis of how environmental monitoring determines what becomes visible, recorded and actionable within governance. It traces the sequence from detection through classification, thresholds and escalation, showing how monitoring design also shapes the evidence available to decision-makers and the public. The paper contrasts systems designed mainly to confirm expected behaviour with architectures that actively test uncertainty and support adaptive reassessment.

Preston New Road: Technical Assessment of Hydrogeological Risk, Monitoring and Governance Limits

Reportv1.1.1, July 2026

A source-based assessment of the assumptions used to govern shale-gas development in a complex and faulted geological setting. It examines hydrogeology, induced seismicity, monitoring design, planning assumptions and regulatory response at Preston New Road. The paper finds that monitoring was largely structured to confirm predicted behaviour, while some plausible alternative pathways and distributed responses remained difficult to test or exclude within the systems used.
